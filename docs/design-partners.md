# Design partner program (GTM)

Goal: three to five design partners — two or three longevity clinics and one
research group — using the product on real files during a fixed pilot window,
in exchange for clearly worded permission to use de-identified aggregate data.
This document is the working checklist and the terms skeleton; it is not
legal advice and the data-terms section requires counsel review before the
first signature.

## Why these partners, in this order

- **Clinics** are the paying segment: they already order methylation and blood
  panels, and are underserved on interpretation. They teach us the vocabulary
  the report must speak.
- **One research group** keeps the scientific posture honest and produces the
  methods credibility (and eventually the replicate samples that replace our
  provisional technical-SD estimates).

## Offer

- Free access for a fixed pilot window (90 days from first processed sample).
- Direct line to the builders; their interpretation questions become the
  roadmap.
- In exchange: signed de-identified aggregate data terms (below) and a
  minimum commitment of real usage (suggested: 10 subjects or 20 samples
  during the window, whichever comes first) plus one feedback session every
  three weeks.

## De-identified aggregate data terms (skeleton for counsel)

1. Partner affirms uploaded files are de-identified at the source: subject
   references are the partner's own pseudonymous codes; no names, DOBs, MRNs,
   or contact information appear anywhere in uploaded files. The platform's
   subject records store only the pseudonymous reference, age, and sex.
2. Partner grants RUBIKS DNA a non-exclusive, perpetual right to use
   **de-identified, aggregated** data — measurements, computed results, QC
   outcomes, and intervention-to-delta episodes — for product improvement,
   reference distribution construction, and aggregate research analyses.
3. Aggregation floor: no external publication of any aggregate in which fewer
   than 10 subjects from a single organization contribute to a cell.
4. No re-identification attempts by either party; contractual, not just
   technical.
5. Partner may terminate forward collection at any time; already-aggregated
   data is not recalled (this is the clause counsel must pressure-test).
6. Raw uploaded files remain the partner's property; export or deletion on
   request at any time (self-serve export already ships in the product).
7. No PHI: partner agrees not to upload identified data during the pilot;
   the platform's PHI gate is enforced in the database, not just the terms.

## The paid interpretation workshop (run before the UI hardens)

The cheapest way to learn clinic vocabulary is to sell interpretation before
selling software:

- **Format:** 90 minutes, remote, clinic's own (de-identified) lab files
  processed into state maps and walked through live.
- **Price:** paid, deliberately — a token fee (hundreds, not thousands)
  filters for clinics that value interpretation, and "paid" converts the
  session from a demo into user research they show up for.
- **Deliverable:** the same versioned PDF report the product issues, plus a
  written half-page of caveats (what was not measurable from their files —
  the missing-stickers argument in prose).
- **Capture:** every question asked in the workshop is logged verbatim;
  recurring questions become report copy or drill-down features. The claims
  boundary applies live: no diagnosis, no treatment advice, reference-relative
  language only.

## Pipeline checklist (per prospect)

- [ ] Qualify: do they already order methylation or comprehensive panels?
- [ ] Workshop booked and paid
- [ ] Workshop delivered; questions logged
- [ ] Data terms signed (counsel-approved version)
- [ ] Org provisioned, first real upload processed
- [ ] Week-3 / week-6 / week-9 feedback sessions
- [ ] End-of-pilot: convert to paid (seats + report packs), testimonial, or
      documented reason lost
