Abstract

DNA is the cell's long-term memory: a polymer that stores how to build proteins and regulatory RNA. It is not the live controller of the body. Day-to-day control sits in networks that read DNA — transcription factors, epigenetics, nutrient sensors, damage signals, and intercellular communication.

Aging is the progressive loss of that controllability. Cells accumulate genomic damage, epigenetic noise, failed protein quality control, weak autophagy, mitochondrial failure, senescent cells, exhausted stem cells, and chronic inflammation. These processes are now grouped as twelve interconnected hallmarks of aging. They appear with age, worsen aging when forced, and can be slowed or partly reversed when targeted.

RUBIKS DNA exists to treat this as an architecture problem, not a wellness slogan. The cube metaphor is exact enough to be useful: many coupled faces, few legal moves, and a scramble that gets harder as constraints stack. The work is to find the short sequences of moves — genetic, epigenetic, and circuit-level — that restore function without collapsing cell identity.

This paper states the mission, the science we accept as established, the research we will pursue, and the claims we refuse to make.

1 Mission

Map the controllable layers of the genome and its read-out, then design interventions that restore cellular function with the fewest, safest moves.

We exist to:

  • Separate stored information (DNA sequence) from runtime control (how that information is used).
  • Build tools that measure where a cell or tissue sits in its functional state space.
  • Design genetic and epigenetic architectures that improve repair, identity, and resilience.
  • Translate only what is mechanistically justified and ethically constrained.

We do not exist to sell immortality, "rewrite your DNA" consumer kits, or a fake number of aging states.

2 What DNA is actually for

DNA is a four-letter archive (A, T, C, G). In humans it is about three billion base pairs per haploid genome. Most of it does not code for proteins. The useful fraction includes:

  • Protein-coding genes — recipes for enzymes, structure, receptors, repair machinery.
  • Regulatory DNA — promoters, enhancers, silencers that decide when and how much.
  • Noncoding RNA genes — molecules that help control other genes.
  • Structural sequence — centromeres, telomeres, replication origins.

The cell does not "run DNA" the way a CPU runs a program. It transcribes selected regions into RNA, translates some RNA into protein, and marks DNA and histones so the same genome can be a neuron in one cell and a hepatocyte in another.

If sequence is a book, aging is both torn pages and a reader who can no longer find the right chapter. Architecting DNA without architecting control is incomplete. Architecting control without respecting sequence constraints is unsafe.

3 Why a cube, not a ladder

Popular aging talk is linear: damage piles up, you decline. That is partly true. It is not the whole picture.

A more useful model:

  • A cell has many coupled variables (repair, identity, inflammation, energy, waste clearance).
  • Those variables form a state space.
  • Healthy function is a compact region of that space.
  • Aging is drift plus loss of the ability to return to that region after stress.
  • Interventions are moves. Some commute. Some do not. Order matters.

This is why the Rubik's metaphor is better than a ladder. You cannot solve one face by smashing another. Full Yamanaka reprogramming can reset age marks and also erase cell identity — a solved cube that is no longer a liver cell. Partial, pulsed reprogramming tries to turn only the faces that restore youth-like function while leaving identity intact. That is the difference between a legal move and taking the cube apart.

We will not publish a precise census such as "4.3 × 1019 states." That number is a branding invention unless derived from an explicit, public model. Combinatorial state spaces are enormous if you discretize many biomarkers. The exact integer is a choice of bins, not a measurement of nature.

4 Scientific foundation (what is already real)

4.1 Twelve hallmarks, not one cause

López-Otín, Blasco, Partridge, Serrano, and Kroemer (2023) updated the hallmarks of aging to twelve:

genomic instability · telomere attrition · epigenetic alterations · loss of proteostasis · disabled macroautophagy · deregulated nutrient-sensing · mitochondrial dysfunction · cellular senescence · stem cell exhaustion · altered intercellular communication · chronic inflammation · dysbiosis

Three tests define a hallmark: it appears with age; making it worse accelerates aging; targeting it can slow or partly reverse aging. They are networked. Hitting autophagy changes nutrient sensing. Hitting senescence changes inflammation. There is no single "aging gene" to overwrite.

RUBIKS implication: a genome architecture program must target coupled hallmarks, or it will solve one face and scramble three others.

4.2 The genome is already an engineering substrate

CRISPR/Cas9 is not science fiction. Casgevy (exagamglogene autotemcel) edits a patient's own blood stem cells to reactivate fetal hemoglobin and is FDA-approved for sickle cell disease and transfusion-dependent β-thalassemia; the label has been extended to young children. That is sequence-level architecture used as medicine — still narrow, still hard, still real.

What this proves:

  • We can make precise, therapeutic cuts in human DNA in defined cell types.
  • Delivery, manufacturing, off-targets, and immune risk remain the bottleneck.
  • Whole-body "redesign the human genome for longevity" is not the same as editing BCL11A in hematopoietic stem cells.

4.3 Control lives above the sequence

DNA methylation clocks estimate biological age from CpG marks. Horvath's pan-tissue clock and later clocks (PhenoAge, GrimAge, pan-mammalian clocks) show that the read-out layer tracks age and, in many studies, mortality risk better than chronological age alone. Clocks are not aging itself. They are a compressed projection of state. They are good enough to treat as instruments, if we do not worship them as the thing being measured.

4.4 Identity can be kept while some age marks move

Yamanaka factors (OCT4, SOX2, KLF4, MYC) can return a differentiated cell to pluripotency. Full induction risks tumors and identity loss. Ocampo, Belmonte and colleagues showed that cyclic, short OSKM expression in a progeria mouse model improved aging hallmarks and extended lifespan without the full pluripotent reset. Transient factor expression in human cells can move epigenetic age and some functional marks while preserving identity. This is the most important existence proof that "solve some faces, leave the others" is biologically possible. It is not yet a human longevity therapy. First-in-human work is being aimed at isolated tissues (for example, the eye) precisely because the blast radius is smaller.

4.5 Nutrient sensing is a control law already in the wild

mTOR, AMPK, insulin/IGF-1, and sirtuins are the body's existing controllers for growth versus repair. Fasting, calorie restriction, and protein/amino-acid restriction change those controllers and raise autophagy. That is why a 75-hour fast is not the same as "clean food in a deficit," and why 18:6 keto is closer than random eating but still not an empty multi-day state. These are control inputs, not DNA rewrites. Any DNA-architecture program that ignores them will over-edit and under-control.

4.6 Synthetic circuits are how you add new control, not just new genes

Mammalian cells can host designed logic: inducible promoters, CRISPR-based transcription factors, feedforward and feedback circuits, multi-input gates. That is cybergenetics: treat gene expression as a plant to be controlled, not a static cassette. This is how "architect DNA" should be interpreted in the medium term — add sensors and limiters, not dump extra genes into every cell.

5 What "architect DNA" should mean here

Four layers. We work them in order of leverage and safety.

Layer A — Measurement architecture

Multi-omic state vectors: methylation clocks, proteomics, senescent-cell burden, mitochondrial function, autophagy flux proxies, inflammatory set points.

Goal: know which face is scrambled before touching the cube.

Layer B — Control-layer engineering (epigenetic / circuit)

Partial reprogramming payloads, CRISPRa/i, synthetic promoters, kill switches, tissue-restricted drivers.

Goal: restore youthful use of existing sequence.

Layer C — Sequence repair and reinforcement

Targeted edits for monogenic drivers, extra copies of repair genes in defined cells, mitochondrial quality-control support, telomere-maintenance only where justified.

Goal: fix torn pages, do not rewrite the novel.

Layer D — System integration

How a move in liver changes immune tone; how mTOR inhibition interacts with a reprogramming pulse; how refeeding after nutrient deprivation completes a repair cycle.

Goal: legal move sequences, not isolated tricks.

The product of this program is not "a new human genome." It is a library of validated moves with known coupling, dose, tissue, and stop conditions.

6 Research agenda

Near term (tools)

  • Publish an explicit, open state model: which variables, which units, which couplings. No invented state counts.
  • Compare interventions by control cost: how much function is restored per unit of risk.
  • Prefer ex vivo and spatially confined in vivo systems (blood, skin, eye, organoids) before systemic payloads.

Mid term (moves)

  • Map which hallmark clusters move together under partial reprogramming, mTOR modulation, and senescent-cell clearance.
  • Build synthetic circuits with hard off-switches and cell-type restriction.
  • Test whether sequence-level repair of high-impact loci plus control-layer reset beats either alone.

Long term (translation)

  • Combination protocols only after single-move toxicity is known.
  • Human studies that treat aging-adjacent disease first (fibrosis, degenerative eye disease, immune aging), not "longevity" as an indication until regulators and biomarkers are ready.

7 Claims we will not make

  • That aging has been counted as 4.3 × 1019 discrete states.
  • That we can upload a solved genome into an adult.
  • That CRISPR approved for sickle cell implies CRISPR for lifespan.
  • That epigenetic clock change equals rejuvenation of the person.
  • That fasting, keto, or supplements are DNA architecture.
  • That more autophagy is always better. Excessive autophagy can destroy tissue.

Honesty is part of the architecture. A cube with hidden stickers is not solved.

8 Ethics and constraints

  • Identity first. A younger cell that is no longer the right cell type is a failure.
  • Containment. Every constructive circuit needs an off switch.
  • No germline consumer product. Somatic, consented, tissue-limited work only unless a separate, publicly justified program exists.
  • Equity of risk. First experiments must not be dumped on people with the least protection.
  • Publish negative couplings. If two moves fight, that is a result.

9 Organizational stance

RUBIKS DNA is a research organization. Its output is models, tools, papers, and — only later — regulated therapeutic programs.

North star metric Increase the fraction of biological state space from which a tissue can still return to function, without losing identity.

That is controllability. That is the solve.

Not every scramble has a short solution.
The work is finding which ones do.
