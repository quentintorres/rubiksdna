02 / Science we accept as established

Twelve hallmarks, not one cause.

López-Otín, Blasco, Partridge, Serrano and Kroemer (2023) define twelve interconnected hallmarks. Three tests qualify each one: it appears with age, forcing it accelerates aging, and targeting it can slow or partly reverse aging. They are networked — there is no single aging gene to overwrite.

  • Genomic instability
  • Telomere attrition
  • Epigenetic alterations
  • Loss of proteostasis
  • Disabled macroautophagy
  • Deregulated nutrient-sensing
  • Mitochondrial dysfunction
  • Cellular senescence
  • Stem cell exhaustion
  • Altered intercellular communication
  • Chronic inflammation
  • Dysbiosis

03 / Moves

Four layers, to be worked in order of leverage and safety.

This is the intended agenda, not completed work. The techniques named below exist in the published literature; none of them are our validated moves. If this program succeeds, its output is not a new human genome — it is a library of validated moves with known coupling, dose, tissue, and stop conditions.

  1. Layer A

    Measurement architecture

    Multi-omic state vectors: methylation clocks, proteomics, senescent-cell burden, mitochondrial function, autophagy flux proxies, inflammatory set points.

    Goal — know which face is scrambled before touching the cube.

  2. Layer B

    Control-layer engineering

    Partial reprogramming payloads, CRISPRa/i, synthetic promoters, kill switches, tissue-restricted drivers.

    Goal — restore youthful use of existing sequence.

  3. Layer C

    Sequence repair and reinforcement

    Targeted edits for monogenic drivers, extra copies of repair genes in defined cells, mitochondrial quality-control support, telomere maintenance only where justified. The most speculative layer, and the easiest to overread — these are open literature ideas we would have to earn, not capabilities we hold.

    Goal — fix torn pages, do not rewrite the novel.

  4. Layer D

    System integration

    How a move in liver changes immune tone; how mTOR inhibition interacts with a reprogramming pulse; how refeeding after nutrient deprivation completes a repair cycle.

    Goal — legal move sequences, not isolated tricks.
